@@ A1 | Understanding requirements

@@ A8 | Software engineering foundations
Which TWO errors should NOT be retried until the request itself is changed? (Choose TWO.)
+ 400 `invalid_request_error`
+ 413 `request_too_large`
- 429 `rate_limit_error` with a `retry-after` header
- 529 `overloaded_error`
- 500 `api_error`
> 400 means the request is malformed or invalid, and 413 means it's too big. Both fail identically on every retry until you fix or shrink the request. 429, 500 and 529 are transient conditions and are retried with backoff (honouring `retry-after` on 429).

@@ S2 | Guardrails and safe deployment

@@ C1 | Session management

@@ C2 | CLAUDE.md hierarchy
A monorepo has `CLAUDE.md` files at the repository root and in `services/payments/`. A developer launches Claude Code from the repository root. How are these files loaded?
+ The root file loads at launch; the subdirectory file loads on demand, and they add together

- The subdirectory file replaces the root file whenever it exists, for the whole session from launch onward
- Both files are merged into `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md` at startup

@@ E1 | Recovery strategies and evaluation
An LLM-as-judge grading support answers consistently prefers longer answers, even when they are less accurate. What is the best fix?
+ Tighten the rubric (correctness first, penalize padding), ask for reasoning followed by a structured verdict, and calibrate the judge against human-labelled samples (or use pairwise comparisons with swapped positions)
- Switch the judge to a smaller model
- Ask the judge to give every answer a score of 7 or above
- Remove the judge and rely on one manual spot check per release
> LLM judges have known biases (verbosity, position, self-preference). A clear rubric, structured verdicts and calibration against human labels make them reliable. Pairwise comparison with position swapping counters position bias. The other options either don't address the bias or throw away scalable evaluation.
